About this dashboard

Results come from equity_gaps_all_levels_long.csv, produced by app_equity_gaps.R from the OfS individualised student data. The population is full-time, UK-domiciled first degree students registered at Middlesex. Only the four academic faculties are shown.

Reading the trend line

Equity gap

The comparison group's rate minus the target group's rate, in percentage points. A positive gap means the target group does worse. BTEC entrants are compared with A-level entrants for continuation, completion and attainment, and with all other qualifications for progression.

Most recent year or 4-year aggregate

Most recent year shows the latest single year for each measure. 4-year aggregate combines the latest four years into one figure, as the OfS does. The aggregate is steadier where student numbers are small, so department and programme views open on it. The baseline is always the 4-year aggregate of the four baseline years.

Which milestone?

Results are compared with the 2026-27 milestone. The APP baselines use the data available when the plan was written, and each later milestone applies to the data available in that year. This file is the 2026 OfS release, which is the data the University holds during 2026-27.

Status

  • Target met: the gap is at or below the 2028-29 target.
  • Significantly ahead of milestone: the gap is below the 2026-27 milestone by more than its margin of error.
  • On track: the gap is at or below the 2026-27 milestone, but within the margin of error.
  • Behind milestone: the gap is above the 2026-27 milestone, but within the margin of error.
  • Significantly behind milestone: the gap is above the 2026-27 milestone by more than its margin of error.
  • Small numbers: either group has fewer than 10 students.

Is the gap to the milestone significant?

This asks whether the distance between an area's gap and the 2026-27 milestone is bigger than chance would produce, given how many students are involved.

Each gap has a 95% margin of error, worked out from the size of each group and its rate. If the gap minus its margin is still above the milestone, the area is significantly behind. If the gap plus its margin is still below the milestone, it is significantly ahead. Otherwise the difference from the milestone is not significant.

The status combines both questions: which side of the milestone the gap falls, and whether the difference is larger than the margin of error. The line under each status shows the distance from the milestone and the margin, so the judgement can be checked.

Tableau calculation: 1.96 * SQRT(pT*(1-pT)/nT + pC*(1-pC)/nC) * 100, where p is each group's rate and n its headcount.

Other equity gaps

This page reads equity_gaps_scan.csv and compares every demographic group in the OfS file with its comparison group, at each lifecycle stage. It looks beyond the APP targets, so new or growing gaps can be researched before the next plan is written.

  • Monitor closely: a significant gap of 10 pp or more over the latest four years.
  • Monitor: a significant gap of 5 to 10 pp.
  • Small but significant gap: a significant gap of under 5 pp.
  • No significant gap: within the margin of error.
  • Too few students: fewer than 20 students in either group over the four years.

These bands are set at the top of section 10 of the R script, and the dashboard applies the same bands to whichever scan file is loaded. The OfS file does not include care experience, estrangement, caring responsibilities, commuting, parental higher education, refugee status or Gypsy, Roma and Traveller communities, so those groups are not covered.

Ethnicity over time

This page reads ethnicity_outcomes_by_year.csv, written by section 11 of the R script, as CSV or Excel. The first chart shows each group's rate. The second shows each group's gap to White students: White students' rate minus the group's rate, so a line above zero means the group's rate is lower.

Each year shows single years. 4-year rolling combines each year with the three before it, so each point rests on four times as many students and moves less from year to year. Hollow markers show points with fewer than 10 students. Select a group's button to show or hide its line.

Largest department gaps

This page ranks departments within each faculty by their largest equity gap, to help choose departments for the reverse mentoring pilot.

  • Each department's gap for each target uses the latest four years combined, adding up students across the years before working out the rates.
  • Each gap is compared with the University's gap for the same target over the same years. A positive difference means the department's gap is wider than the University's.
  • Targets for the same group of students are combined into one figure for that group, weighted by the number of students in the target group for each. The four BTEC targets become one BTEC figure, and the two free school meals targets one free school meals figure, so each group counts once alongside ethnicity and deprivation.
  • Departments are ranked by the group with the widest gap. The student mentor's background follows from that group.
  • A target is left out where either group has fewer than 20 students over the four years.
  • ! marks a gap that is wider than the University's by more than its 95% margin of error. For a combined group, the margin comes from the margins of each target, weighted in the same way.

The page opens on the 4-year aggregate. Most recent year ranks on the latest single year instead, compared with the University in the same year, and leaves out targets where either group has fewer than 10 students. Single years rest on far fewer students, so their margins of error are much wider.

Small departments are more likely to top a list by chance, because their gaps move more. The option to count only significant gaps ranks departments on the gaps where the difference is larger than the margin of error.

Report builder

The report builder puts sections from any page into one PDF, in the order you choose.

  • Drag a section type from Add a section into the list, or select it to add it to the end. Drag sections in the list, or use the arrows, to change the order.
  • Each section has its own options (the cog): which area, target and view to show, which parts to include, and whether it starts on a new page.
  • On any other page, the download button on each panel can also add that panel, or the whole page, to the report with the filters already chosen.
  • Select any title, heading, caption, summary or introduction in the preview to edit it. Changing a section's options resets the edited headings inside that section, but keeps its title and introduction. This includes switching between the most recent year and the 4-year aggregate, so an edited summary never quotes the other view's figures.
  • Area summaries, Target by area, Progress map and Largest department gaps sections have a switch under their title for the most recent year or the 4-year aggregate, and Ethnicity over time sections for each year or 4-year rolling. The switches are not printed.
  • Download PDF opens the print window. Choose Save as PDF. Pages are A4 landscape.
  • The layout and edited text are kept in this browser, so the report is still there next time. Load the results files again to fill it. Save layout and Open layout move a layout between computers or keep several versions. A layout file holds no results.

Student numbers
